# Levels.fyi

## What it does

Collects and publishes compensation and job-levelling data for technology roles, contributed by the workers whose compensation it describes.

## How it started, which is the part that matters here

In 2017, as a static website covering three companies, with Google Forms collecting submissions, Google Sheets holding them, and AWS S3 serving the page. The founders put the annual infrastructure cost at less than a cup of coffee.

First traffic came from answering questions people were already asking on Blind and similar forums, with a link to the answer. No audience, no advertising, no reputation.

## Why this is the best-matched source company in the corpus

The founder's constraints for this project are zero capital, twenty hours a week, one person, wholly online, no audience, no standing. Almost every company examined in five prior runs fails at least one of those at its own starting point, which forces `economic_model` down and leaves candidates resting on mechanisms proven only under conditions the founder cannot reproduce.

Levels.fyi's *starting* conditions are reproducible almost exactly. Its *current* state — roughly 48 employees, 3 million monthly visitors — is a real ceiling. It is small enough that the mechanism is visibly doing the work, and large enough that the work clearly went somewhere.

## What made it work

The people who wanted the data were the people who had it. Each individual's own compensation is nearly worthless alone and the aggregate is valuable enough to trade for.

Verification is the unglamorous half. Self-reported salary data drifts upward unless checked, and an inflated dataset is unsellable to the employers who fund it.

## What does not transfer cleanly

Two founders, not one, from the beginning. The current scale runs on 48 people and two institutional investors, so nothing here shows the mechanism scaling far without either. And the verification burden grows with the dataset while a solo founder's hours do not.

## Open questions

At what dataset size the employer side becomes willing to pay, which is the number a new entrant most needs and the one nowhere reported. How long the gap was between launch and first revenue. Whether the 2020 figure of 60,000 dollars ARR represented three years of accumulation or a late start to monetisation.

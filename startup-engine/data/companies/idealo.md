# Idealo

## What it does

Aggregates product and price data across merchants so a buyer can find the cheapest available source for a specific item, then sends the buyer to the merchant.

## How it makes money

Merchants pay per click on outbound traffic. Revenue scales with click volume rather than with completed sales, which decouples the operator's income from the merchant's conversion rate.

## What made it work

The information is ingested, not written and not contributed. Once a merchant integration exists, the data refreshes itself — prices update without the operator doing anything. This is the most automatable of the three variants.

The cost moves to merchant acquisition and feed integration. Every merchant is a separate commercial agreement and a separate technical integration, which is relationship and operations work rather than content work.

Note on independence: Idealo operates under a larger media group. It is independent of the other instances recorded here, but it is not an independent company in the ownership sense, and it may have benefited from group distribution. That distinction matters when counting corroboration and should not be glossed.

## How it differs from other instances

Against NerdWallet and G2: the comparison is objective and machine-readable. There is no editorial judgment to produce and no community to moderate. The information problem is an integration problem.

The monetization differs from both — per click, not per conversion and not per subscription. That pays earlier than a lead fee but requires substantial traffic volume before it amounts to anything.

Its cold-start problem is merchant supply, not audience and not content.

## Open questions

Revenue is unsourced and may not be separately disclosed. Whether the model reached viability on its own or on the back of group distribution is `UNKNOWN`, and it is the question that determines whether this variant is replicable by a solo operator at all.

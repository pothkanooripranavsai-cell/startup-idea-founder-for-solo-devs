# G2

## What it does

Collects reviews of business software from verified users and organizes them into comparable categories for buyers evaluating a purchase.

## How it makes money

Software vendors pay — subscriptions for enhanced presence, category placement, and access to buyer-intent signals. Reviewers and buyers pay nothing.

The payer is the party being reviewed, which creates a structural tension the operator has to manage credibly.

## What made it work

The information is contributed rather than produced, so the corpus compounds without proportional editorial cost. The cost is moved to the front: a review site with no reviews is worthless to buyers, and vendors will not pay until buyers arrive.

Seeding that first corpus is the hard part, and it is usually done by leaning on existing industry relationships. Whether that was the case here is `UNKNOWN` and is the single most important open question for anyone considering this variant.

## How it differs from other instances

Against NerdWallet: the information is user-generated, not editorial. Lower marginal content cost, but a genuine cold-start problem and an ongoing moderation and verification burden that NerdWallet does not have.

Against Idealo: the unit of comparison is subjective experience, not an objective price. There is no feed to ingest and no merchant integration, but also no automatic refresh — the corpus ages unless users keep contributing.

Monetization is the most different axis: recurring vendor subscriptions rather than per-conversion fees, which means revenue does not require the buyer to transact at all.

## Open questions

Revenue is unsourced. Whether the initial review corpus was seeded through pre-existing vendor or buyer relationships is unsourced and would directly determine whether Gate 3 passes for any adaptation of this variant.

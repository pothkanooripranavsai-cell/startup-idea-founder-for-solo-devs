# ScrapingBee

## What it does

Sells one annoying technical capability — retrieving web pages reliably despite blocking, proxies and browser rendering — as a metered API that developers call instead of building and maintaining it themselves.

## How it makes money

Usage-based pricing, self-serve, no sales team. Developers arrive through search and documentation, evaluate by reading, and convert without speaking to anyone.

## Why it is in this corpus

It is the ambition profile the founder asked for from a company in the right band: bootstrapped, tiny team, roughly 5 million ARR, eight-figure exit. Not a side project, not a category leader carried by capital.

The distribution mechanism is the genuinely transferable part. Documentation and technical content accumulate and keep ranking; buyers self-identify by searching for the exact problem. No audience, no reputation, no outbound selling — the same structural advantage `public_corpus_aggregation` has, arrived at from a different direction.

## What does not transfer

Founder-market fit is explicitly credited in the sources, and this founder is a generalist by constraint. Five years and several failed projects preceded the outcome, so the fifteen-month acceleration describes a late stage rather than a starting point.

The hazard a candidate must handle independently is cost ordering. Serving a request costs money — proxies, compute, bandwidth — and free tiers and trials incur that cost before any conversion. This is the same shape `ev_020` used to close the Atlassian Forge path. Usage-based pricing does not resolve it by itself; prepaid credits with no free tier might.

## Open questions

What the gross margin actually is after infrastructure cost, which decides whether the model is viable at small scale or only above a volume threshold. Whether a generalist can pick the annoying task correctly without the domain familiarity the founders had.

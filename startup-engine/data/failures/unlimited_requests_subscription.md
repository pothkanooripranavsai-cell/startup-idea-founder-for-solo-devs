# Flat-fee subscription promising unlimited requests

## What it looks like

One flat monthly fee, submit as many requests as you like, work returned on a queue. Recurring revenue, payment in advance, no per-item negotiation, and the operator controls throughput by working the queue at their own pace.

Under this founder's constraints it is close to the most attractive-looking shape available, and it scores well on exactly the dimensions this project weights most: revenue before cost, bootstrap feasibility, and a clean answer to Gate 8.

## What actually goes wrong

The company that pioneered it abandoned it, and said why in its own founder's words: there is no such thing as unlimited. Prospects can request without limit; the operator can deliver a finite amount per day. The distance between those two facts is filled by disappointed customers.

It also degraded as a category. Competitors copied the promise, the promise became routinely undeliverable across the industry, and the framing itself acquired a bait-and-switch reputation — so a new entrant now inherits the distrust without having personally earned it.

## Why a solo operator is worse placed than the originator

Design Pickle absorbed the mismatch with staff in the Philippines, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Vietnam, India and Indonesia, adding throughput as demand grew.

This founder cannot hire before revenue and has a twenty-hour committed week. The identical promise is therefore backed by the smallest possible capacity. The mechanism does not shrink to fit — it breaks more visibly.

## What survives, and what does not

The **subscription with a queue** is fine. Recurring revenue for recurring work, paid in advance, delivered on a cadence, is a sound structure and is essentially what a well-specified retainer has always been. Design Pickle's own replacement — explicit pricing tied to stated hours and scope — is that.

The **unlimited framing** is what failed and is closed. A candidate may propose a capped, explicitly scoped recurring service. If a future candidate proposes unlimited requests at a flat fee, this record is the reason to reject it rather than research it further.

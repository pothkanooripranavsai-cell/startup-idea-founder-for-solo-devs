# Building a product and launching it to an audience the founder does not have

## What it looks like

Pick a problem, build a small paid product, launch it, post about it. The revenue figures are real, publicly disclosed, and verified against a stated methodology. The businesses are genuinely solo, genuinely online, genuinely unfunded.

It is the most obvious answer to the question this engine asks, and every list of one-person internet companies points at it.

## What actually goes wrong

The launch is the visible step and it is not the load-bearing one.

These products sell on day one because a large number of people already follow the founder and have watched them work for years. Strip that away and the same product, built to the same standard, reaches nobody. `ev_019` records the founders describing this themselves, in their own words, as the deliberate strategy it was.

The founder constraints here specify no audience, no reputation, no following, zero standing. Against that, this class of business is evidence for a mechanism the founder cannot operate.

## Why it is recorded rather than noted

Because it will keep presenting itself as a candidate. It is well documented, the numbers are large, the operators are solo, and the businesses are wholly online - it clears every surface test this engine applies and fails on a precondition that is invisible in the source material.

Any candidate proposing a self-launched product must be scored against `ev_019` on the `economic_model` dimension, where the 0 anchor reads: the mechanism only works with capital, a team, or an existing audience; strip those and nothing is left.

## What is not being claimed

Not that these businesses are illegitimate, nor that audience-building is a bad strategy in general. Only that it is a multi-year unpaid input, that it must be paid before revenue, and that a founder at zero standing with a twenty-hour week has not got it and cannot buy it.

## The useful contrast

`remote_rocketship` is the same shape - solo, online, unfunded, bootstrapped - and is not subject to this failure, because its distribution is produced by the asset it assembles rather than supplied by the founder. That distinction, not the solo-versus-funded one, is what separates transferable mechanisms from untransferable ones here.

A primary source, which is rare in this corpus, and it closes a path that looked unusually promising.

## Why the path looked good

An established platform marketplace appears to solve the constraint that has blocked candidates across three runs. The platform supplies the buyers, who are already paying customers of the platform and therefore have budget and a procurement route. The platform supplies the trust, so the operator's lack of reputation does not enter the transaction. The platform handles billing. Listing is free. And from January 2026 a developer keeps all revenue up to a million dollars lifetime, which is a real and recent change rather than a standing condition.

On Gate 7 and on the standing problem this is close to ideal.

## Why it closes anyway

Consumption is billed to the developer above the free allowance, and the billing does not care whether the app earns anything. A free tier user, a trial user, and a paying user all consume the same quota and only one of them pays.

Gate 8 asks about order, not size: it fails a model where costs arrive structurally ahead of revenue. Here they can, and the mechanism that makes them do so is adoption - the very thing the operator is trying to cause. Success and cost are correlated while success and revenue are only partly correlated.

Gate 1 is survivable during validation if usage stays inside the free allowance. Gate 8 is the harder problem and it is structural rather than a matter of being careful.

## The second objection

Revenue arrives only after an app exists. There is no pre-sale, no manual delivery, no interim signal. Against a rubric that reserves its top bootstrap anchor for candidates that can be paid before anything is built, a platform app is close to the opposite shape.

## Recorded so it is not revisited

Over two thousand apps are listed and fewer than twenty developers have passed a million dollars in direct sales. The distribution is extremely long-tailed. This record exists so a later run recognises the shape quickly rather than researching it again.

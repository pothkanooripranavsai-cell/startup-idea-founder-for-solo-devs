This is the canonical real-world proof for the mechanism `presold_outcome_service` has carried since run 02 with zero recorded instances. It is also the most famous "fake it before you build it" story in startup writing, which is exactly why it earns the caution below rather than an uncritical citation.

## Why this is the mechanism proof the family lacked

Every candidate built on `presold_outcome_service` across four runs was scored against a family record stating `company_count: 0`. That is not a minor gap — a mechanism with no observed instance cannot support a `solo_transfer` score above `null`, and run 04's idea_16 was correctly left unscored for exactly this reason.

Zappos is the instance. Test whether a market wants an outcome delivered a specific way, by delivering it manually and by hand before building anything that scales, and only build the infrastructure once real orders prove the demand. That is `presold_outcome_service`'s mechanism stated exactly.

## Why this is classified `mixed` rather than `supports`

Zappos itself fails two of this founder's constraints outright, and pretending otherwise would misuse the record.

**It is not online-only in the relevant sense.** Swinmurn drove to physical stores and shipped physical shoes. The *validation* method (photograph, list, manually fulfill) is doable online; the *product* was not digital.

**It did not stay zero-capital.** He lost money on every manual sale — buying at retail, selling near cost — and the model only became viable once venture capital funded inventory and infrastructure. The concierge phase proved *demand*; it did not prove the business could self-fund from that point forward, which is exactly what Gate 8 asks and exactly where this record cannot support a PASS on its own.

## What transfers and what does not

What transfers cleanly: the sequencing insight — deliver the outcome by hand, at a loss or at cost if necessary for the test, before spending anything on infrastructure. That sequencing is available at zero capital when the "manual fulfillment" is founder time on a digital deliverable rather than founder time plus wholesale cash outlay on physical goods.

What does not transfer: any assumption that the model self-funds past the concierge phase the way Zappos did. Zappos needed outside capital to cross from proof-of-demand to durable business. A candidate citing this record must independently establish that its own manual-delivery phase is not, like Zappos's, structurally loss-making.

## How to use this record

Cite it for the *sequencing* claim only — outcome sold and delivered manually before automation exists is a proven-to-work pattern, at the scale of "acquired for 1.2 billion dollars." Do not cite it for solo-operability, zero-capital sustainability, or online-only delivery, none of which the instance itself demonstrates.

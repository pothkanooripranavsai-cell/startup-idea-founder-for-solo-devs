The single most consequential record added in run 04, and it is a negative one.

## What it disqualifies

Lists of successful one-person internet companies are the obvious place to look for mechanisms a solo operator can run, and this project has been circling them. `PhotoAI` at 138K monthly, `ShipFast` at over a million annually, `HeadshotPro` at 3.6M annually, `TypingMind` at 130-160K monthly. Each is genuinely solo, genuinely online, genuinely unfunded.

Each is also a product launched to a large pre-existing audience by a founder who spent years acquiring it.

The founder constraints in this project specify no audience, no reputation, no following, and zero standing. Against that constraint, these businesses are evidence for a mechanism the founder cannot run. The product is visible; the decade of distribution-building that made the launch work is not.

Marc Lou states the strategy in his own words: he began posting so that "if I can get a few people watching my work, I won't have to start from 0 when I launch new startups." Eight months to the first thousand followers. That is the cost of the input, and it is paid before the mechanism produces anything.

## Why this is recorded as evidence rather than as a note

It sets the `solo_transfer` score for an entire class of candidate. The rubric's 0 anchor reads: "the mechanism only works with capital, a team, or an existing audience; strip those and nothing is left." Build-a-product-and-launch-it, evaluated honestly against a zero-audience founder, sits at 0 or 1 on that anchor regardless of how well documented the revenue is.

Any future run that proposes a micro-SaaS, an AI tool, a boilerplate, or a paid digital product launched by the founder to the public must clear this record first or be marked down by it.

## What survives it

Mechanisms whose distribution comes from something other than the founder's standing. `ev_017` and `ev_018` describe one: an asset assembled from public data that ranks in search on its own merits, where the operator's reputation is not part of the transaction.

The distinction is not solo versus funded. It is whether the audience is an input the founder must supply or an output the mechanism produces.

## Caution on the aggregator source

The list this line of enquiry started from states its inclusion rule plainly - solo-led, with revenue publicly disclosed by the founder at a citable URL - which is a real methodology and better than most sources here. It says nothing about audience, and a summary asserting these businesses are audience-independent is contradicted by the founders' own accounts. The inclusion rule verifies revenue. It does not verify transferability.

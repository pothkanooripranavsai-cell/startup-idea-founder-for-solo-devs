Added by the deep research pass on `idea_13` to attack the unknown that had been carried unresolved against the whole `public_corpus_aggregation` family: whether collecting public entries is permitted.

It is resolvable, it has a determinate answer, and the answer is more useful than a yes or a no.

## The two halves, which point opposite ways

The statutory question resolves favourably. A public page with no access permissions cannot be accessed "without authorisation", so the CFAA does not reach it. Three independent firms report the same holding; they share the underlying ruling and therefore count as one source, not three.

The litigation resolved unfavourably for the scraper anyway. hiQ took a 500000 dollar judgment, conceded liability under trespass to chattels and misappropriation, and was enjoined from further scraping. Winning the CFAA question did not save it.

Marked `mixed` for that reason. Anyone quoting only the first half is quoting half a case.

## What actually distinguishes the two outcomes

Not the act of collecting. The relationship with the source.

hiQ was taking from a single platform that objected, had sent a cease and desist, and asserted contract and property interests in its own compiled database. The exposure was contractual and tortious, and it attached to the *counterparty*, not to the activity.

That distinction is directly operable. Collecting from a source that publishes openly, requires no account, imposes no click-through agreement, and *wants the material read* is a materially different position from collecting a competitor's compiled database behind an accepted agreement.

`ev_017` records the evidenced instance taking from employer career pages and applicant tracking systems — sources whose publishers post openly in order to be found, and whose interests the collection serves rather than opposes. That is the favourable side of the line.

## The operating constraint this produces

Take from primary publishers. Never from another aggregator's compilation.

This is not a hedge. It is the difference between the position that won on the statute and the position that lost on everything else.

## Not relied upon

Commentary on whether individual job postings attract copyright — the general view being that a single posting lacks the originality required while a compilation of them may be protected, and that click-through agreements bind independently of copyright — comes from attorney question-and-answer sites and informal legal blogs. That is community tier, which under `rules/evidence_policy.md` cannot sole-support a FACT.

It is recorded because it points the same way as the holding above and because a later pass should know it was seen. It is not counted as support, and the specific terms of service of specific applicant tracking systems were not read in this pass. The claim that collection from primary publishers is permissible therefore remains INFERENCE, bounded by a real precedent rather than resting on one.

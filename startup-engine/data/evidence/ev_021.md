Subcontracting to agencies was examined in run 04 as a way to borrow someone else's standing. The agency holds the client relationship and the credibility; the operator supplies delivery capacity behind it.

The appeal is real. It removes the end client from the trust problem entirely.

## Why it does not remove the problem

It relocates it. The agency becomes the buyer, and the agency's stated selection criterion is a track record of agency delivery - precisely the thing a founder with no standing does not have. A closed list of trusted partners is a network, and Gate 3 exists to catch exactly that.

The bar is also rationally higher than an end client's, because the agency is reselling the work under its own name and absorbs the reputational cost of a defect. That argument does not depend on the sources' motives, which is why this is INFERENCE rather than a discarded promotional claim.

## Why it is not classified FACT

Both sources sell the service they are describing and benefit from freelancers appearing unreliable. No source was found from the buying side, from a neutral observer, or from a solo provider describing how they actually entered. The claim is plausible and structurally supported but it is not measured, and `ev_011` is the standing precedent in this corpus for refusing to promote a self-interested account.

## What would settle it

An account from a solo operator who entered agency subcontracting from a standing start, describing what the agency actually required. None was found. Until one is, a candidate built on this route carries Gate 3 as an unresolved risk rather than a cleared one.

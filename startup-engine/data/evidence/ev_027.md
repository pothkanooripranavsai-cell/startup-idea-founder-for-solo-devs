The mechanism behind `ev_026`'s outcome, recorded separately because the origin story and the business model are different claims and a candidate may need one without the other.

## The exchange

The people who want the data are the people who have the data. Each individually holds one data point that is nearly worthless alone — their own compensation — and collectively wants the aggregate badly enough to give theirs up for access to it.

That inverts the cold-start problem every prior family in this project has fought. `niche_listing_board` needs an audience before suppliers pay. `public_corpus_aggregation` needs a corpus assembled from public sources before anyone subscribes. Here the first contributors and the first customers are the same people, and the asset is built by the demand side rather than for it.

## Why the moat compounds rather than plateaus

Each contribution makes the dataset more useful, which attracts more contributors. 245,000 data points across 5,000 companies (`ev_026`) is not a corpus a competitor can scrape, buy or parse into existence — it exists only because hundreds of thousands of individuals chose to hand it over, one at a time, to the party that had already accumulated enough to be worth contributing to.

This is the compounding property the founder's ambition preference asks for, and it is the first mechanism in this corpus that has it structurally rather than as a hoped-for side effect.

## Why verification is load-bearing, not a detail

Self-reported data about money invites inflation, and a dataset known to be inflated is worthless to the employers who pay for it. Verification through offer letters and pay portal screenshots is what makes the aggregate sellable at all.

For a solo operator this is the hidden cost: verification is manual, unglamorous, recurring, and cannot be skipped without destroying the asset. Any candidate adapting this mechanism must account for that load rather than assuming the dataset assembles itself.

## Two-sided monetisation, one-sided contribution

Individuals contribute and consume free. Employers pay for benchmarking and access. That ordering matters — the side being asked to pay is not the side being asked to contribute, which avoids taxing the behaviour the whole mechanism depends on.

## Not established here

Revenue figures beyond an early 60,000 dollar ARR datapoint from 2020, which is stale and far below current scale. How long the dataset took to become sellable. Whether the employer side would pay at a materially smaller dataset size — which is precisely what a new entrant would have.

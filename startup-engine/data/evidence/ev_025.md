The category-leader proof for the same distribution mechanism `ev_024` shows at solo scale. G2 is a real, large, independently verifiable company — over 100 million dollars ARR as of 2024, a business already in this corpus from run 01 — which makes this a genuine "reverse-engineer a proven big company's mechanism" record, not an indie-hacker anecdote dressed up as one.

## The mechanism

A participant (here, a reviewed software vendor) is given a shareable artifact — a badge showing their score — that they have a direct incentive to display on their own site, because it is favourable to them and costs them nothing. Every visitor to every vendor's site who sees that badge is a potential new G2 visitor, recruited without G2 paying for the impression or reaching that visitor directly.

The 2025 awards cycle quantifies the effect: 15 million social impressions and a 30 percent traffic spike, attributed to badge-sharing and backlink-driven referral flows rather than paid acquisition.

## Why this record and `ev_024` are read together

Neither is sufficient alone. G2 proves the mechanism works at real scale but was never run solo or from zero capital — it took venture funding and years to build the review corpus and vendor relationships the badges are attached to. RankInPublic proves a solo operator can run the *mechanic* from zero, but at far smaller scale and over a much shorter observed period.

Together they establish what this record set is usually built to establish: a mechanism proven at scale by a funded company, and independently shown to be operable by an unfunded individual. That is the standard this project has been trying to meet since run 01 and has met only once before, for `public_corpus_aggregation` via Remote Rocketship.

## Caution on the specific numbers

The impression and traffic-spike figures trace back to G2's own announcement of its own awards program's performance, relayed by a secondary analysis site rather than measured independently. They describe G2's outcome, not any solo operator's, and are not evidence that a new, unknown badge-loop product would replicate anything close to this scale. They establish that the mechanism compounds at scale when it works — not that it reliably works for a new entrant.

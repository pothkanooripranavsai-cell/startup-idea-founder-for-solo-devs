The best-sourced evidence record in the corpus, and the reason is the underlying source rather than the relays.

SHRM is a professional body publishing benchmarking research, not a vendor selling a product whose value depends on the number being impressive. Every other record gathered across three runs traces to content marketing. This one does not, which is why it is the only claim carrying this weight.

Both relaying organisations cite the same SHRM report, so they share an underlying source and count as one, not two.

What it resolves: the previously unknown assumption that a vacancy lasts long enough to be worth paying to cover. A median of 44 to 54 days settles that decisively. A gap of six to eight weeks is long enough for the work not done to matter.

## Not relied upon

The same sources carry cost-of-vacancy figures — roughly 500 dollars per day for a tech role, 24,000 dollars over a 48-day fill, 72,000 dollars for a 150,000-dollar role left open eight weeks. These come from recruiting firms whose business depends on vacancies appearing expensive, carry no stated methodology, and are derived by dividing salary by working days rather than measured.

They are recorded here so a later pass knows they were seen and deliberately not used. The duration claim stands on its own without them.

A second source in the founder's stated band - a real company with a team, bootstrapped, with a genuinely large outcome - for a mechanism this project has not examined: sell one annoying technical capability as a metered API, and acquire buyers through documentation and search rather than through selling.

Marked `mixed`, because the two halves of this record point in opposite directions for a generalist founder.

## The favourable half

The distribution model requires no audience, no reputation and no outbound selling. Developers with a specific problem search for it, find documentation that solves it, and self-serve onto a metered plan. That channel compounds the way `public_corpus_aggregation`'s does — content and documentation accumulate and keep ranking — without requiring an audience the founder does not have.

The ceiling is real and independently attested: approximately 5 million ARR and an eight-figure all-cash exit, with a tiny team. This is the ambition profile the founder's constraints ask for, from a company small enough that the mechanism rather than capital is doing the work.

The 7,000 dollars MRR to nearly 1 million ARR in fifteen months figure shows the curve is steep once the acquisition engine catches, which is characteristic of compounding rather than linear channels.

## The unfavourable half, which is the reason for `mixed`

**They picked an audience they already understood.** That is founder-market fit, explicitly credited in the sources as a cause of the outcome. This founder's constraints specify a generalist with no industry standing, and nothing in this record establishes that the same mechanism works without the domain familiarity the founders brought.

**It took roughly five years and several failed projects to get there.** The record describes a path, not a repeatable fifteen-month result, and the fifteen-month acceleration happened after years of accumulated learning and an existing product.

## The structural hazard a candidate must address

Metered infrastructure APIs carry real per-request cost — proxies, compute, bandwidth — incurred when a request is served, including on free tiers and trials. That is the same shape as `ev_020`'s finding on Atlassian Forge: adoption generates cost while only conversion generates revenue.

A candidate adapting this mechanism does not inherit a Gate 8 pass from this record. It must independently establish that its own cost is incurred after payment rather than before, for instance through prepaid credits with no free tier — and must say so explicitly rather than assuming usage-based pricing resolves the ordering by itself.

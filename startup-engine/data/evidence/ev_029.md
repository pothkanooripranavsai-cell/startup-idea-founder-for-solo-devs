A primary source, and an unusually clean one: the company that pioneered a model explaining, in its own founder's words, why it stopped running it.

## Why this closes a path rather than complicating one

The unlimited-requests flat-fee subscription is close to the most attractive-looking mechanism available under this founder's constraints. Recurring revenue, no per-unit pricing negotiation, payment in advance of work, and a queue the operator controls. It would have scored extremely well on `revenue_before_cost` and on bootstrap feasibility, and some version of it was likely to be generated in a future run.

The company that invented it abandoned it, and the stated reason is structural rather than incidental: capacity is finite, the promise implies it is not, and the gap between the two is where trust is destroyed. That gap does not shrink at solo scale — it widens, because a single operator's capacity is the smallest possible while the word "unlimited" makes the same promise regardless.

## Why it is worse for a solo operator than it was for Design Pickle

Design Pickle absorbed the mismatch with staff across multiple countries, adding throughput as demand grew. A solo founder cannot. Under this project's constraints paid help is unavailable before revenue, so the only variables are the founder's own hours — already capped at twenty committed — and the honesty of the promise.

A solo operator selling unlimited work is therefore selling something they can deliver less of than the company that found the promise undeliverable at far greater capacity.

## What survives

Not the whole model. The subscription-with-a-queue structure is fine; the *unlimited* framing is what failed. Design Pickle's replacement — explicit pricing tied to stated hours and scope — is a viable shape and is essentially what a well-specified retainer has always been.

A candidate may propose a capped, explicitly-scoped recurring service. It may not propose unlimited requests, and if it does, this record is the reason to reject it rather than research it further.

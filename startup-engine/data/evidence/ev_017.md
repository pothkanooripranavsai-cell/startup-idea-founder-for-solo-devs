This record answers the question `data/model_families/niche_listing_board.md` left open across two runs: whether listing supply can be assembled before any buyer pays.

It can. The supply side is not sold to at all.

## What the mechanism actually is

Job openings are already published, in structured form, on a small number of applicant tracking systems that thousands of companies use. The openings are scattered across thousands of company career pages but the underlying format is uniform.

The operator collects them, parses out the fields buyers care about - salary, location eligibility - and sells filtered access to the people who want the jobs.

No employer is contacted. No employer relationship exists. No employer has to be persuaded of anything.

## Why this changes the constraint picture

Every prior candidate in this project that adapted this mechanism needed either an audience before the supply side would pay, or a supply side before the audience would come. This instance has neither dependency. The supply is public, free, continuously renewed by parties acting for their own reasons, and structurally uniform enough to parse.

`ev_006` established that roughly ninety percent of independent consultants get their first client through a network. This mechanism does not route around that problem the way `ev_012` does, by finding buyers who advertise themselves. It removes the problem, because the thing being sold is assembled from public data rather than from relationships.

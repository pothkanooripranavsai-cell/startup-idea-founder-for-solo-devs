# Exchange-for-access data network

## Mechanism

Find information that is widely held in tiny private fragments and widely wanted in aggregate. Each holder's fragment is nearly worthless alone. The aggregate is valuable enough that holders will trade their fragment for access to everyone else's.

The operator supplies neither the data nor an audience. They supply the place where the trade happens, and the credibility that makes the aggregate worth trusting.

## Why it answers the founder's ambition preference structurally

Every other family in this corpus returns roughly linearly to founder effort. One more listing, one more digest, one more outreach email, one more manual delivery.

This one does not. Each contribution makes the dataset more useful, which attracts contributors, which makes it more useful again. `ev_027` records the result: over 245,000 verified submissions across more than 5,000 companies, an asset that cannot be scraped, purchased or parsed into existence because it exists only as the accumulated result of individual decisions to hand it over.

That is a genuine moat rather than a hoped-for one, and it is why this family is the strongest available answer to a constraint set that forbids capital, audience and team.

## Why it also inverts the cold-start problem

`niche_listing_board` needs an audience before the supply side pays. `public_corpus_aggregation` needs a corpus assembled before anyone subscribes. `public_budget_signal` needs buyers to convert from a cold approach.

Here the first contributors and the first customers are the same people. The asset is built by the demand side. `ev_026` records how the evidenced instance triggered that loop from nothing: answering questions people were already publicly asking, with a link to the answer.

## The four conditions a domain must satisfy

Drawn from one instance, so a hypothesis about the family rather than a finding:

1. **Fragmented** — the information is held individually, in small pieces, by many people.
2. **Wanted in aggregate** — each holder wants everyone else's pieces more than they mind giving up their own.
3. **Safe to give** — anonymity or aggregation makes contributing low-risk. Where contributing exposes the contributor, the loop does not start.
4. **Verifiable** — self-reported data drifts unless checked, and an untrusted aggregate is unsellable.

Condition three is what most candidate domains fail on, and condition four is what most operators underestimate.

## Requirements

Capital: effectively none to start.

Expertise: low, though identifying a domain that satisfies all four conditions is the whole skill.

Network: none, which is the point.

The real recurring cost is verification. It is manual, unglamorous, does not diminish with scale, and skipping it destroys the asset. A solo operator with twenty committed hours has to plan for it explicitly.

## Open questions

**At what dataset size the paying side becomes willing to pay.** This is the number a new entrant most needs and it is nowhere reported for the evidenced instance. A dataset far below 245,000 points may be worth nothing to an employer, in which case the unpaid accumulation phase is long and its length is unknown.

Whether the mechanism works in domains where contribution is less socially normalised than compensation sharing has become.

Whether one person can carry the verification load at all past a threshold, or whether it forces hiring — which the evidenced instance did.

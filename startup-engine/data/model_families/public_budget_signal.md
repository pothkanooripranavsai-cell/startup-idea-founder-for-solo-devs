# Sell against a public declaration of approved budget and unmet need

## Mechanism

Most businesses begin by finding an audience and hoping some of it has money and a problem. This inverts that: start from parties who have already announced, publicly and in writing, that they have money and a problem.

The archetypal signal is an open role. A job posting requires budget approval, headcount planning and leadership sign-off, so by the time it appears publicly the money is allocated (`ev_012`). It is also timestamped, free to read, and continuously refreshed.

Other signals share the shape: vendors paying for placement on a marketplace, publicly dissatisfied paying customers, announced funding, published grants. All are public statements that budget exists and a need is unmet.

## Why it pays

Every candidate across the previous two runs failed or stalled on the same constraint — reaching buyers with no audience, no reputation and no advertising budget. `ev_006` quantified the difficulty: roughly ninety percent of independent consultants get their first client through a pre-existing network.

This mechanism does not solve that problem so much as route around it. The buyer list is published by the buyers themselves. It requires no audience to assemble, no corpus to seed, no data to purchase, and no relationships to inherit. It costs nothing and renews daily.

The substitution is real: organisations do buy an outcome as a service instead of hiring for it, particularly when they need the expertise but not full-time, or cannot find the right person quickly (`ev_014`).

## How instances differ

**No instances are recorded.** `revenue_evidenced_count` is 0. What exists is strong evidence that the *signal* works and that the *substitution* happens, not a sourced operator who has built a business this way. That gap must not be glossed.

The axes that would distinguish real instances:

**Signal type** — an open role, a marketplace placement, a public complaint, a funding announcement. Each implies a different urgency and a different buyer.

**Signal freshness** — the sources agree a posting is worth far more in its first days than weeks later (`ev_013`). Whatever is built must operate inside a narrow window.

**Substitution versus addition** — whether the offer replaces the thing the buyer was going to buy, or sits alongside it. Replacing a planned hire is a threatening proposition; covering the gap until the hire lands is not. This axis probably decides conversion more than anything else.

**Delivery load** — whether the outcome sold consumes founder hours proportionally to clients, which determines whether the ceiling arrives quickly.

## Requirements

Capital: none. The signals are free.

Network: none for *finding* buyers, which is the whole point. Standing may still matter for *converting* them, and `ev_004` and `ev_006` both suggest it does.

Expertise: entirely determined by the outcome sold, not by the mechanism.

## Open questions

Whether a buyer acting on a public signal will engage an unknown solo provider rather than an established firm. This is the same standing problem that has shadowed every run, relocated rather than removed.

Whether the vendor-published conversion multiples in `ev_013` mean anything. They are recorded as INFERENCE precisely because they may be marketing.

Whether operating inside a narrow freshness window is compatible with a twenty-hour week.
